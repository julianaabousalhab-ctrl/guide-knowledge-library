# Root Issues Map — "Sunday 9:30 pm" Routing

The taxonomy organizes the library by area of practice. This map organizes it the other way — by the question a stuck Guide actually types. Each root issue routes to the BrainLifts that answer it.

Two notes on how to read this. First, the map is **illustrative routing, not exhaustive coverage** — the taxonomy remains the authoritative index, and a BrainLift absent from this map is not unreachable, it just answers a narrower variant of a routed question. Second, the final section lists the questions this library deliberately does **not** answer, because they need policy or ops documents, not coaching patterns.

---

## 1:1 coaching and academics

### "My student barely did anything on the platform today. What do I do tomorrow?"

Check it against the student's baseline before treating it as a problem — **BL-089** documents a student who "looked off task" while ahead of pace, and the discipline of checking data before correcting. If it's a real repeating pattern, **BL-096** gives the tomorrow-morning move: fixed-cadence data review with numeric triggers that open a flagged case automatically. If the flag fires, **BL-102**: a "Behind" flag is not an intervention — record the barrier and the booked response at the moment of flagging.

### "They're stuck on this concept and keep getting it wrong — how do I help without teaching the lesson?"

**BL-032**: watch the student attempt the task before diagnosing — performance data alone can't distinguish the barrier. **BL-113**: rule out tool illiteracy first; it masquerades as a learning gap. Then **BL-041**: classify the barrier as strategy, instructional fit, or system failure — each gets a different response, and re-teaching is rarely right for any of them. If it's structural, **BL-126**: route it to the named DRI with an evidence package instead of compensating harder in the classroom.

### "The student says they'll do it and then doesn't — and I never saw it coming."

**BL-116**: score every promise from both sides; divergence between the Guide's and the student's read is the diagnostic trigger. **BL-033**: co-create and confirm the accountability agreement rather than announcing it. **BL-036**: when the miss happens, pair the consequence with a clear repair pathway.

### "Am I focusing on the right things in 1:1s?"

The Coaching & Accountability category *is* the answer as a set: goal discovery (**BL-028/BL-029/BL-114**), anchored reflection (**BL-030**), probing (**BL-031**), action planning (**BL-037/BL-038**), and follow-up with evidence (**BL-042**). The through-line: every meeting ends with something specific, owned, and checkable.

## Motivation

### "They're disengaged and just clicking through — how do I re-motivate them?"

**BL-086**: identify the driver (mastery, autonomy, competition, belonging) before selecting a lever. **BL-070**: once an interest is named, decode what it *delivers* for the student and rebuild that property in the work — don't theme the work or dangle the interest as a prize. **BL-066**: if the lever is a reward, match it to the student's demonstrated value system. If the student is stalled on open-ended work specifically, **BL-128**: structure the choice — assigned core plus student-chosen remainder. And if there's a warm relationship but no system, **BL-121**: a relationship is not a motivational system; install a named structure and enforce it without fear of losing the rapport.

### "My reward system worked for a month and died."

**BL-069**: move the motivational bar in both directions — static bars decay whether the student is coasting or drowning. **BL-080**: treat every model as scaffolding — launched against a diagnosed gap, with its retirement designed in. **BL-109**: refresh every student's plan on a staff-day cadence rather than waiting for visible failure.

### "Students are gaming XP / racing through checks."

**BL-108**: write the quality condition into the earning rule so gaming is structurally unprofitable. **BL-084**: frame XP as balanced learning progress, not point farming. **BL-112**: multipliers steer effort — they don't create it; use them to rebalance subjects, not as the engine.

### "Prizes are escalating and it's getting expensive."

**BL-081**: build the reward ladder from earned status, autonomy, and responsibility instead of prizes — two campuses' own model rationales document the damage of prize-based regimes.

## Behavior and wellbeing

### "My student refused to work all afternoon — what if it continues tomorrow?"

**BL-077**: walk the student to the posted standard and let them classify their own behavior — verdict-delivery to an escalated student is the documented anti-pattern. **BL-074**: move regulation upstream; teach signal recognition and pre-select the tools before the moment. **BL-087**: turn the trigger into a reset-and-return routine, co-created in conversation. **BL-095**: whatever the response, calibrate it across Guides — students track per-Guide drift precisely.

### "There's a conflict between students — who handles what?"

**BL-075**: separate the students, take individual accounts, corroborate with uninvolved witnesses — first accounts predictably omit the speaker's own contribution. **BL-076**: make the first parent message complete (facts, actions taken, what happens next); the corpus records the trust cost of a material fact arriving late. **BL-078**: after the incident, change the conditions, not only the student.

### "The student shuts down when I correct them."

**BL-018**: validate the student's perspective before challenging it. **BL-019**: adjust the approach when the student signals a mismatch. **BL-098**: once a delivery sequence works, write the tested feedback protocol into the profile so every adult uses it — not just the one who discovered it.

## Relationships and transitions

### "I'm inheriting another Guide's students."

**BL-115**: run the transition as a relationship handoff — rapport before coaching, assets before agenda, walk the inherited record with the student. **BL-129**: student knowledge is team property — interests, strategies, and seams travel to every adult who shares the student. **BL-098**: the documented feedback protocol travels with the profile.

### "A new student joined midyear and isn't integrating."

**BL-091**: induct them into *owning* the community — a role and a contribution — rather than handing them a finished system to fit into.

### "We have great rapport but no output."

**BL-124**: trust is working capital — once built, the coached next move is to spend it on higher standards, using the relationship as the reason the student stretches. **BL-121**: the relationship itself is not the intervention; every flagged student gets a named structure.

## Whole-group sessions

### "My Launch fell flat — kids did the activity but nothing landed."

**BL-008**: name the exact behavior or strategy students should repeat — the transfer moment is a naming moment. **BL-013/BL-014**: have students name a personal context and action, then embed immediate use in the next authentic task. **BL-017/BL-044**: close with a concrete cue for the next context and a named time for the next attempt.

### "Students give up the moment the Launch gets hard."

**BL-001**: build a second attempt into the design and require a strategy change between attempts. **BL-002**: make mistakes recoverable so students stay in the task. **BL-099**: probe past persistence language — students absorb "keep trying" on their own but not "change your approach"; the revision half needs deliberate transmission.

### "Half the group is just watching during the Launch."

**BL-009**: give every student an interdependent role. **BL-010**: when participation must be sequential, give observers an active job — evidence, comparison, or feedback, not just cheering.

### "Townhall votes go nowhere / I end up doing all the talking."

**BL-048**: lock one clear decision before opening the vote. **BL-050**: resolve ties with a protocol students already understand, agreed before the tie. **BL-051**: start recurring topics from the current adopted rule instead of relitigating. **BL-055/BL-056**: clarify and return the question to students; intervene firmly on process, then immediately hand leadership back. **BL-057**: match support to readiness — and remove it on purpose.

### "Townhall is student-led on paper but I'm still running it."

**BL-045**: build the agenda before the meeting with a student name beside every topic. **BL-046**: give student leaders a defined area of real authority, not ceremonial titles. **BL-047**: move community work from adult reminders to student-owned roles.

### "Our shout-outs have gone generic."

**BL-118**: teach the shout-out as a form — model its anatomy, give every student a turn, upgrade vague praise live. **BL-097**: ritualize mistake-sharing so failure has a recurring, celebrated slot. **BL-111**: hand the daily closing itself to students with rotating leadership and a fixed structure.

## Workshops and assessment

### "How do I set up a workshop so I can prove growth later?"

**BL-103**: baseline the life skill in week one — progress judgments need a recorded start. **BL-106**: decompose before you design — skill → subskill → "I can" behaviors, activity last. **BL-071**: publish student-facing pass criteria on day one; ambiguity reads as a motivation problem.

### "Lots of students failed the AlphaTest — now what?"

**BL-090**: mass failure is a design signal — trigger a system review, not N individual remediations. **BL-104**: every individual "not passed" closes with the component-level gap and a booked response. **BL-088**: before the next one, rehearse under test conditions — guided success does not transfer on its own.

### "Students perform for me but I don't trust my own grading."

**BL-127**: externalize the bar — real experts set and judge the AlphaTest, coached to rigor, blind where possible. **BL-105**: score with cited evidence and cap the fixes at three. **BL-120**: design peer-feedback instruments so specificity is structural — balanced, required comments beat deficit-triggered checkboxes.

## Parent communication

### "A parent emailed upset that their child isn't learning anything — how do I respond?"

**BL-058**: structure the reply Reality → Lever → Next Step; stopping after "reality" is the named anti-pattern. **BL-064**: if the real issue is invisible progress, communicate trajectory — earlier state, what changed, current direction — never a bare "making progress." **BL-065**: end with a measurable checkpoint (action, measure, timeframe). **BL-062**: describe observable behavior, never character. For serious news, **BL-079**: stakes, plan, and evidence-based belief in one message.

## Guide workload and development (lead-guide questions)

### "I feel behind on check-ins, notes, and follow-ups — what's 'good enough'?"

**BL-125**: the answer in the corpus is structural, not motivational — every guide runs a self-built weekly operating system with named responsibilities scheduled into it, so nothing recurring depends on memory. **BL-043**: individual commitments get tracked to closure. **BL-123**: pre-scripted probes and quotas convert "don't rescue" from a principle into countable reps — which also caps unplanned time spent doing students' work for them.

### "My guide keeps rescuing students / talks like a psychologist / drowns in follow-ups."

The Guide Portal corpus makes this a answerable category: **BL-123** (train the hold-back as countable reps), **BL-122** (coach the guide's vocabulary — plain words in, adult labels out), **BL-125** (install the weekly operating system). These are lead→guide coaching patterns — the library answers questions asked *about* Guides, not only *by* them.

## Questions this library deliberately does not answer

These recur in Guide questions but are policy or operations, not coaching patterns — they need an ops document with named contacts and current rules, and would go stale inside a BrainLift. Listed so the gap is a decision, not an oversight:

- **Mental-health escalation** — "who do I contact tonight" requires a current escalation roster, not a pattern.
- **Attendance procedure** — official process ownership sits outside the Guide knowledge domain (see taxonomy Not-in-Scope).
- **Cheating / AI-misuse procedure** — same: needs the current policy, not historical practice.
- **What Guides may approve vs. what needs leadership review** (schedule changes, exceptions) — authority boundaries are set by Admin, not derived from transcripts.
- **Guide performance minimums and recognition** — belongs to the talent system, not this library; the Guide Portal corpus documents *coaching* toward these, which is as far as the evidence goes.
