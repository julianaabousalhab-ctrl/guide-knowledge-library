# Root Issues Map — "Sunday 9:30 pm" Routing

The taxonomy organizes the library by area of practice. This map organizes it the other way — by the question a stuck Guide actually types. Each root issue routes to the BrainLifts that answer it. The final section lists the questions this library deliberately does **not** answer, because they need policy or ops documents, not coaching patterns.

---

## "My student barely did anything on the platform today. What do I do tomorrow?"

Check it against the student's baseline before treating it as a problem — **BL-089** documents a student who "looked off task" while ahead of pace, and the discipline of checking data before correcting. If it's a real repeating pattern, **BL-096** gives the tomorrow-morning move: fixed-cadence data review with numeric triggers that open a flagged case automatically. If the flag fires, **BL-102**: a "Behind" flag is not an intervention — record the barrier and the booked response at the moment of flagging.

## "They're stuck on this concept and keep getting it wrong — how do I help without teaching the lesson?"

**BL-032**: watch the student attempt the task before diagnosing — performance data alone can't distinguish the barrier. **BL-113**: rule out tool illiteracy first; it masquerades as a learning gap. Then **BL-041**: classify the barrier as strategy, instructional fit, or system failure — each gets a different response, and re-teaching is rarely right for any of them. If it's structural, **BL-126**: route it to the named DRI with an evidence package instead of compensating harder in the classroom.

## "They're disengaged and just clicking through — how do I re-motivate them?"

**BL-086**: identify the driver (mastery, autonomy, competition, belonging) before selecting a lever. **BL-070**: once an interest is named, decode what it *delivers* for the student and rebuild that property in the work — don't theme the work or dangle the interest as a prize. **BL-066**: if the lever is a reward, match it to the student's demonstrated value system. If the student is stalled on open-ended work specifically, **BL-128**: structure the choice — assigned core plus student-chosen remainder. And if there's a warm relationship but no system, **BL-121**: a relationship is not a motivational system; install a named structure and enforce it without fear of losing the rapport.

## "My student refused to work all afternoon — what if it continues tomorrow?"

**BL-077**: walk the student to the posted standard and let them classify their own behavior — verdict-delivery to an escalated student is the documented anti-pattern. **BL-074**: move regulation upstream; teach signal recognition and pre-select the tools before the moment. **BL-087**: turn the trigger into a reset-and-return routine, co-created in conversation. **BL-095**: whatever the response, calibrate it across Guides — students track per-Guide drift precisely.

## "A parent emailed upset that their child isn't learning anything — how do I respond?"

**BL-058**: structure the reply Reality → Lever → Next Step; stopping after "reality" is the named anti-pattern. **BL-064**: if the real issue is invisible progress, communicate trajectory — earlier state, what changed, current direction — never a bare "making progress." **BL-065**: end with a measurable checkpoint (action, measure, timeframe). **BL-062**: describe observable behavior, never character. For serious news, **BL-079**: stakes, plan, and evidence-based belief in one message.

## "There's a conflict between students — who handles what?"

**BL-075**: separate the students, take individual accounts, corroborate with uninvolved witnesses — first accounts predictably omit the speaker's own contribution. **BL-076**: make the first parent message complete (facts, actions taken, what happens next); the corpus records the trust cost of a material fact arriving late. **BL-078**: after the incident, change the conditions, not only the student.

## "I feel behind on check-ins, notes, and follow-ups — what's 'good enough'?"

**BL-125**: the answer in the corpus is structural, not motivational — every guide runs a self-built weekly operating system with named responsibilities scheduled into it, so nothing recurring depends on memory. **BL-043**: individual commitments get tracked to closure. **BL-123**: pre-scripted probes and quotas convert "don't rescue" from a principle into countable reps, which also caps unplanned time spent doing students' work for them.

## "Am I focusing on the right things in 1:1s?"

The Coaching & Accountability category *is* the answer as a set: goal discovery (**BL-028/BL-029/BL-114**), anchored reflection (**BL-030**), probing (**BL-031**), action planning (**BL-037/BL-038**), and follow-up with evidence (**BL-042**). The through-line: every meeting ends with something specific, owned, and checkable.

## "The workshop is going badly / lots of students failed the AlphaTest"

**BL-090**: mass failure is a design signal — trigger a system review, not N individual remediations. **BL-104**: every individual "not passed" closes with the component-level gap and a booked response. **BL-071**: if criteria were never published student-facing, that's the root cause, not grit. **BL-088**: rehearse under test conditions before the real one.

## Questions this library deliberately does not answer

These recur in Guide questions but are policy or operations, not coaching patterns — they need an ops document with named contacts and current rules, and would go stale inside a BrainLift. Listed so the gap is a decision, not an oversight:

- **Mental-health escalation** — "who do I contact tonight" requires a current escalation roster, not a pattern.
- **Attendance procedure** — official process ownership sits outside the Guide knowledge domain (see taxonomy Not-in-Scope).
- **Cheating / AI-misuse procedure** — same: needs the current policy, not historical practice.
- **What Guides may approve vs. what needs leadership review** (schedule changes, exceptions) — authority boundaries are set by Admin, not derived from transcripts.
- **Guide performance minimums and recognition** — belongs to the talent system, not this library; the Guide Portal corpus documents *coaching* toward these, which is as far as the evidence goes.
