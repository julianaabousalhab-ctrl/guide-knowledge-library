# Anchor Goal Meetings to the Parents' Stack-Ranked Pillars

## Pattern

Before the goal meeting, the family records how they rank the three pillars for their child — love school, learn 2x, life skills. The Guide (or DoP) then writes one concise, evidence-bearing progress statement per pillar and orders both the write-up and the meeting by the family's ranking, leading with their Rank 1.

The practice has three parts:

1. **Obtain the ranking first.** The family's stack rank is collected and recorded per student before progress is written, not assumed from the school's defaults.
2. **One narrative per pillar, in their order.** Each ranked pillar gets a single dense statement built to the BL-059 evidence standard — a student quote, a named app or behavior, a specific moment — so the parent hears proof, not adjectives, against each of their priorities.
3. **Lead with their Rank 1.** The meeting opens on the pillar the family cares about most, even when the school's biggest win sits lower in their ranking.

The mechanism: families genuinely diverge on priority — in the recorded data, learn-2x-first and love-school-first families are nearly equal in number, with a substantial life-skills-first minority. Any fixed reporting order therefore opens on the wrong priority for roughly half the room. Anchoring to the family's ranking makes the same progress data land as "you are watching what I care about."

Related: BL-061 governs the Guide-chosen priority (the highest-leverage growth area); this pattern governs the parent-chosen ordering of the report. A strong goal meeting does both: ordered by the family's ranking, with the Guide's one-gap focus inside the relevant pillar. BL-059's Name-Swap Test applies to every per-pillar narrative.

## Evidence

*1- The practice executed at seventeen campuses*

The SY25-26 Parents' Stack Ranked Pillars workbook contains a sheet per campus (17 campuses), recording per student: the family's stack rank and per-pillar narratives written against that ranking (Rank 1 / Rank 2 / Rank 3 columns), with an embedded instruction to "write a concise, 1 sentence" statement. 197 students have recorded stack ranks; 148 have at least the Rank-1 narrative completed.

*2- Families genuinely diverge on Rank 1, so ordering carries information*

Across recorded rankings, learn 2x leads for ~40 families, love school for ~37, life skills for ~18 (plus scattered variants) — no single default order matches even half of the families.

Completed Rank-1 narratives are specific and student-unique in the BL-059 sense — e.g., a Rank-1 statement documenting a student who "used to avoid her Fast Math app, but now she wants to tackle it," closing with the student's own words ("I'm good at this!").

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is running every goal meeting in the school's default order — typically academics first — so a love-school-first or life-skills-first family hears their top priority as an afterthought, and reasonably concludes the school is optimizing something other than what they asked for.

*1- Prevalence, from this corpus*

The gap is the majority case, not the exception: of 484 students listed across the 17 campus sheets, 288 (59%) have no usable stack rank recorded ("No data" or blank) — meaning most families' progress communication is necessarily structured on an ordering they never chose. Since rankings in the recorded minority split nearly evenly between learn-2x-first and love-school-first, a default order opens on the wrong priority for roughly half of those families.

*2- The default format, from the library's own corpus (cross-corpus)*

The Session Snapshot comments evidenced throughout Parent Partnership (BL-058–BL-065) are structured by academic content — XP averages, subject gaps, next steps — with no reference to the family's pillar priorities; they document what default-ordered parent communication looks like in practice.

Note: no recorded parent reaction to default-ordered versus rank-anchored communication exists in either corpus, so the cost claim rests on the ranking-divergence data rather than observed parent outcomes.

## Reusable Coaching Move

Use the Rank-Anchor Check before any goal meeting or session write-up:

1. **Do I have this family's current stack rank?** If not, collecting it is the first agenda item — not a skipped step.
2. **Does each ranked pillar have one dense, evidence-bearing statement?** (Quote, named behavior, or specific moment — BL-059 standard.)
3. **Does the meeting open on their Rank 1?**
4. **Is the Guide's next-step focus (BL-061's one gap) stated inside the pillar where it belongs?**

Frame for the opening:

> "You told us [Rank 1 pillar] matters most for [Student] — so let's start there. Here's what we're seeing…"

## Confidence: Medium — a multi-campus executed practice (17 campus sheets, 197 rankings, 148 narrative sets), but the corpus records the school side only; no parent responses or meeting outcomes are captured.
