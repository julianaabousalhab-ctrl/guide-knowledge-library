# Hand the Daily Closing to Students — Rotating Leadership Plus Goal Reflection, Peer to Peer

## Pattern

The daily closing works harder when students run it. The converged practice across four guides' records has two components:

1. **Rotating student leadership of the ritual itself** — students lead the closing discussion, bringing their own content: one student researched a focus strategy, showed it to the group, then ran a whole-class exercise to test it. The rotation is a recurring micro-leadership rep ("more variety, good micro leadership opportunity, more fun relative to normal closing").
2. **A goal-reflection structure inside it** — every student answers whether they met their daily goals; a miss triggers "why," then a plan for tomorrow ("what they could have done differently"). Run daily, the reflection migrates peer-to-peer: "conversations morphing from the guides offering suggestions, to the students offering tips and tricks to each other."

The mechanism: the closing is the day's natural reflection point, and it's cheap — it happens anyway. Making students the operators converts it from an adult wrap-up into a daily rep of two life skills at once (leading a group, evaluating your own day), and the peer-sourced strategy exchange carries more weight than the same tips from a guide.

Related: this is the daily-cadence sibling of the Townhall ownership patterns (BL-046's bounded real authority, BL-057's scaffold-and-fade) and shares BL-097's insight that culture lives in the structure of recurring slots. The goal-reflection component runs BL-042's revisit-with-evidence loop at day-scale. Populates the taxonomy's planned 6.3.2 node.

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is the guide-run wrap-up: the adult summarizes the day, offers the suggestions, and students receive the closing rather than operate it.

*1- The before-state, documented comparatively in-corpus*

Both primary instances record the guide-led default as the lesser state they moved away from: Clark's entry frames student-led closings against the "normal closing" they replaced; Dahm's records the shift from "guides offering suggestions" to peers doing so as the improvement worth reporting.

## Reusable Coaching Move

1. **Put the closing on a student rotation** — a named leader per day/week, with real control over content (their strategy, their activity), scaffolded then faded per BL-057.
2. **Keep the goal-reflection spine constant:** met your goals? If not — why, and what's tomorrow's plan?
3. **Route suggestions sideways:** when a student names a miss, ask the room before offering your own fix — the migration from guide-tips to peer-tips is the health metric.
4. **Protect the tone:** the closing ends the day on the community's energy, not on an adult summary.

## Confidence: Medium — four instances across four guides in one campus-year, with the peer-migration effect documented; single era (SY18-19), not yet re-confirmed under the current model.
