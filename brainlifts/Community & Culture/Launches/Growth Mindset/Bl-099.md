# Probe Past Persistence Language — Students Absorb Only Half of Growth Mindset

## Pattern

When students explain growth mindset in their own words, they overwhelmingly describe the persistence half — "never give up," "push through," "believe in yourself" — and almost never the strategy half: changing the approach after a failure. The revision component that BL-001 builds into Launch design and BL-003 converts into next moves does not survive transmission on its own.

The Guide response is a probing move: treat fluent growth-mindset language as an unverified claim, not as understanding.

1. **When a student uses growth-mindset language** ("I'll keep trying," "failure is fuel"), ask the strategy question: *"Trying again — with what change?"*
2. **When checking understanding** (in a Launch debrief, reflection, or 1:1), ask for the student's definition and listen for both halves. A persistence-only answer is a signal to teach the revision component explicitly, not a pass.
3. **Count growth mindset as understood** only when the student can name both: keep going *and* change something — and ideally point to a moment they did it.

The mechanism: persistence language is emotionally satisfying and socially rewarded, so it spreads on its own; strategy revision is cognitively demanding and spreads only when required. A student whose whole model is "try again harder" will repeat a failing approach with more effort — the exact anti-pattern BL-001 exists to prevent.

Related: This documents the transmission failure that BL-001 (require a strategy change between attempts) and BL-003 (convert the phrase into a concrete next move) are designed to fix. Use those moves as the teaching response when the probe finds a persistence-only definition.

## Evidence

*1- At scale, students define growth mindset as persistence; strategy revision is nearly absent*

Across 1,324 free-text answers to "What does growth mindset mean to you?" (Sessions 2–4, 15+ campuses), persistence phrasing dominates — by conservative keyword match ~24% use "keep trying / never give up / push through" formulations, and manual reading of random samples shows persistence, self-belief, and positivity carrying most of the remainder. Only ~1% (15 of 1,324) mention a strategy, approach, or "different way"; ~4% use "yet."

Representative persistence-only answers:

> "it's to tell yourself keep trying"

> "To use a strong brain and tell yourself I am strong and I am never giving up"

> "Growth Mindset means to keep going"

*2- The complete definition exists in the population — it is just rare*

A small minority of students state both halves unprompted, showing the target definition is learnable at these ages:

> "To not give up and if I get something wrong, I will try again and try a new strategy."

> "Trying different strategies for work when I get stuck."

> "Saying 'I will try another way' or 'I can not do this yet'"

Source artifact:[Student Session Survey](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1k7j-Oxkw-61FFc8GFFb7LleZ5kkVD-D-AlKRrrE-k-o/edit?usp=drive_link)

*3- Self-reported know-how vastly exceeds strategy-aware understanding*

In the same sessions, 88–95% of students answer Yes to "When you face a challenge, do you know how to use a growth mindset to get through it?" The gap between near-universal claimed know-how and ~1% strategy-aware definitions is the measurement of the transmission failure.

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is accepting persistence language as evidence that growth mindset landed — praising "never give up," logging the life skill as demonstrated, and moving on.

The survey data indicates this is not an occasional lapse but the default outcome of current teaching at scale: students echo the emotional vocabulary all year while the actionable half stays missing. The cost surfaces later as students who respond to failure with repetition instead of revision (see BL-001's anti-pattern: a retry with no strategy change).

## Reusable Coaching Move

Use the Both-Halves Check whenever growth mindset comes up:

1. **Elicit:** "What does growth mindset mean to you?" or, in the moment, "Okay — growth mindset. What does that look like right now?"
2. **Listen for the second half.** Persistence-only answers get the follow-up:

> "Keeping going is half of it. What will you *change* on the next try?"

3. **Anchor it to the live task:** have the student name the specific adjustment (new order, smaller section, different method, targeted question) before they re-attempt — the BL-003 move.
4. **Close the definition:** "So growth mindset is two things — you keep going, *and* you change your approach. Which half did you just use?"

## Confidence: Medium — 1,324 responses across three sessions and 15+ campuses point one direction, but the corpus is a single instrument type (student self-report), and the coaching response is grounded in BL-001/BL-003 rather than observed in this source.
