# Score Every Promise From Both Sides — Divergence Between Guide's and Student's POV Is the Diagnostic Trigger

## Pattern

A guide's read of a student and the student's self-report are two different instruments, and neither is authoritative alone. The strong practice scores each promise (love of school, 2x, life skills, guide relationship, culture) from both sides, side by side — and treats every mismatch as a case to investigate, never as a number to average or a side to trust by default.

Investigated divergences resolve into three distinct outcomes, each with a different response — all three documented in the corpus:

1. **A real signal** the guide's read missed — the student's "No" is true and specific ("he doesn't believe that a teacher or anyone in a similar role can possibly change someone's life"), and the response is a targeted plan.
2. **A misread question** — the divergence was measurement error ("misunderstood the question in S2 apparently"), and the response is fixing the instrument, not the student.
3. **Invalid data** — the self-report wasn't sincere ("he put dishonest answers that don't reflect his true feeling"), and the response is fixing the survey conditions.

The closing move in the documented practice: every investigated divergence gets a written root cause and a next-session improvement plan in the same record.

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is letting a standing divergence sit — the student's "No" recorded session after session while the guide's "Yes" (or simple inaction) rides alongside it, with nobody assigned to reconcile the two.

*A "No" that persisted across a guide generation before anyone root-caused it*

> "The entire year, even before I was his guide, Alyan has said 'no' for Love of School."

The signal was in the data across multiple sessions and survived a guide transition untouched; only the current guide's investigation surfaced the actual root (volatile friendships, a believed threat of being pulled from Alpha) and produced a belonging-based plan. Every session the divergence sat uninvestigated was a session the plan didn't exist.

## Reusable Coaching Move

Use the Score-Both → Flag → Investigate → Write process each session.

### 1. Score both sides independently

Record your POV on each promise before looking at the student's self-report, so the two instruments stay independent.

### 2. Flag every mismatch — in both directions

Student-No/Guide-Yes is the obvious case; Guide-No/Student-Yes matters equally (you may be underrating a student who's fine). No divergence is "small enough" to skip — the flag costs nothing.

### 3. Investigate before interpreting

A follow-up conversation, not an inference, decides which of the three outcomes you're holding: a real signal (build the plan), a misread question (fix the instrument), or invalid data (fix the survey conditions). Expect repeat follow-ups on standing divergences — one conversation often isn't enough.

### 4. Write the root cause and the plan in the same row

Close every investigated divergence with a written root-cause analysis and a next-session plan, in the shared record — so the flag can't outlive a guide transition unexamined (see BL-115).

The coaching standard is not:

> "The Guide tracks the promises and reviews the student surveys."

The standard is:

> "Every promise carries two scores — the Guide's and the student's — every mismatch has been talked through, and each one ends in a written root cause and plan rather than a standing contradiction."

## Confidence: Medium-High — the dual-POV instrument is in live use at scale (107 divergent rows, 101 written root-cause + plan entries) and three guides across two campuses documented divergence investigations resolving into all three outcomes (real signal, misread question, invalid data), with an observed anti-pattern (a year-long uninvestigated "No" that survived a guide transition); capped below High because the POV columns are template design (the codified craft is only the investigation-and-closure discipline layered on them), and no outcome data yet links investigated divergences to improved next-session scores.
