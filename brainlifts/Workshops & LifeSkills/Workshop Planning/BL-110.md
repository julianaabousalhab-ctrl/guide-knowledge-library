# Give the Work a Real Client — External Stakes Produce Professional Behavior No Simulation Matches

## Pattern

When the workshop's activity context includes a real external client — a company, a working artist, a paying customer — students' output acquires properties that guide-invented scenarios don't produce: professional constraints get honored, revision gets accepted, and motivation runs on the client's judgment rather than the guide's.

The executed forms, seven years apart:

1. **A student-run consulting firm with booked clients** (SY18-19): students applied with pitch videos, then delivered for Warby Parker (a SXSW store event), DNV GL (a public blockchain explainer), Disney+ (ad campaigns), Kammok and Hey Cupcake — "they get to see the fruit of their labor used to change the world around them. Motivation and excitement around this company is extremely high."
2. **A branding agency serving real musicians** (SY25-26): student teams ran discovery consultations with working Austin artists and built their EPKs — including honoring a professional constraint by removing YouTube uploads at the band's request "to avoid interfering with an upcoming release, showing real-world industry awareness."
3. **Real transactions for student products**: a guide purchasing a nine-year-old's actual essential-oil product line from his Build-a-Business check.

The client also supplies the revision pressure the guide would otherwise have to manufacture: cold-outreach emails "sent back to the drawing board" until the subject line worked ("12-year-olds have ideas for Disney Plus"); project timelines rejected "several times until they had a workable timeline."

Related: this is the strongest version of BL-106's "activity that naturally forces the behaviors" and BL-004's audience frame — the audience is real, so the frame never breaks. BL-014's authentic-task principle at workshop scale; BL-035's real-responsibility logic applied to the whole activity context.

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is audience-less work: deliverables whose only reader is the guide, revised only because the guide asks.

*1- Documented replacement decisions*

The corpus records guides abandoning the audience-less default rather than measuring its failure: the traditional research paper was explicitly replaced with public-facing formats — "in place of the traditional research paper… find and follow experts on Twitter, produce two blog posts per week… and prepare for a TED talk" — because the private format lacked the "robust and valuable research and retention process" the public one produced.

## Reusable Coaching Move

When choosing the activity context (BL-106 step 3), run the Client Test:

1. **"Who outside this building needs this work?"** — a business, an artist, a community org, a real buyer. If nobody, can the deliverable be made public (published, performed, sold) so an audience exists?
2. **Let the client set the constraints** — deadlines, revisions, and professional norms come from them; the guide translates, never replaces.
3. **Let the client's judgment be the bar** — acceptance, purchase, or use in the world is the pass condition the T2P wraps.
4. **Keep the guide's role to preparation** (BL-088's rehearsal, BL-105's draft feedback) — the client provides the stakes; the guide provides the readiness.

## Confidence: Medium — three independent executed instances across two eras and different guide teams, with the revision-pressure mechanism documented; no controlled contrast against client-less equivalents exists.
