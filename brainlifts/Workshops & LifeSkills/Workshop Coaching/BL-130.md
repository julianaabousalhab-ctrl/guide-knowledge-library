# Split the Project 90/10 — Students Own the Work and Its Fair Failures; the Adult Owns the Failure Modes Students Can't Control

## Pattern

In real-world student projects (external partners, deadlines, physical logistics), one lead guide runs an explicit ownership split, named as "90% student led / 10% adult":

1. **Students own the work and its fair failures** — outreach, forms, stocking, presenting; if they under-execute, they fail on their merits and the failure stands.
2. **The adult owns the failure modes students can't control** — shipping lead times, nonprofit paperwork (501(c)(3) numbers), backup plans, adult-to-adult institutional outreach. This work is done *outside* the student project: "adult to adult. This is not part of their project."
3. **The test distinguishing the two**: would the failure teach the student something about their own execution, or only about logistics they never controlled? The lead states it directly — a deadline miss caused by shipping times is not a failure the students should own.

The split protects real stakes (BL-110 territory) without letting uncontrollable logistics convert a student success into a fake failure — or an adult rescue convert a fair failure into a fake success.

## Anti-Pattern

Two symmetric failures of the unsplit project: (a) purism — the adult touches nothing, and students eat a "failure" caused by shipping windows, missing paperwork, or an unresponsive institution; (b) rescue — the adult quietly does student-owned work to guarantee the outcome, and the pass stops meaning anything. (The rescue side is the workshop-scale cousin of the reflex in BL-123.)

## Reusable Coaching Move

Before a real-world project launches, and again at each checkpoint:

1. **List the failure modes.** For each: could the students have prevented it by executing better?
2. **Yes → student side.** It stays in the project; if it happens, it's a fair failure and it stands.
3. **No → adult side.** Logistics, institutional paperwork, backup plans, adult-to-adult calls — handled outside the project, invisibly to the pass/fail judgment.
4. **Say the split out loud** — "90/10" gives the guide team shared language for whether a given intervention is legitimate.

## Confidence: Medium — the split is named and applied with a clear boundary in one rich transcript, and the same lead runs the adult share consistently across three other guide projects; but all instances are one lead guide (Samantha DePalo), and the "90/10" formulation appears once. Raises with a second lead running an explicit ownership split, or a documented case where the adult share saved a project without touching the student-owned judgment.
