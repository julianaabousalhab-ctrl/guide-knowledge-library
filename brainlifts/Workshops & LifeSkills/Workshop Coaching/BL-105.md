# Score With Cited Evidence, Cap the Fixes at Three — the Draft-Feedback Format That Makes Revision Executable

## Pattern

When scoring student work at a draft checkpoint, the strong Guide feedback carries three structural features, every time:

1. **Every score cites evidence from the artifact itself.** The number never stands alone — it quotes what earned it and names what's missing, verbatim from the student's work ("the site URL/name is 'echo nova,' while the band name shown is 'Noise Devoid'"; "the Music section has no tracks/links… Contact has no visible email").
2. **The fix list is capped at three and ordered by importance.** Not everything wrong — the three actions that most move the score, each concrete enough to execute without interpretation ("embed at least 1–3 demos/live recordings"; "add a visible booking email + socials — don't rely only on a form").
3. **The same rubric scores the draft and the final.** Draft and AlphaTest feedback use identical domains and scale, so revision is aimed at named dimensions and progress is visible on them (drafts scoring 1–2/4 on Communication/Creativity, finals reaching 3/4 on the same domains).

The mechanism: an evidenced score teaches the rubric (the student sees what the number means in their own work); a capped, ordered fix list converts assessment into a plan sized to the revision window; a stable rubric makes the second scoring a measurement instead of a new opinion. Together they make draft feedback function as BL-088's midpoint checkpoint is meant to — preparation quality assessed and corrected before the final event.

Related: This is the artifact-feedback sibling of BL-008/BL-026 (name the specific behavior behind the judgment) and applies BL-061's prioritization logic to revision (three ordered actions here vs one growth area in a parent comment — different budgets for different windows). ## Evidence

*1- Evidenced scoring at the draft stage, across teams*

All six First Draft Feedback documents (five distinct real-client artist teams) score Communication and Creativity with artifact-quoted evidence, including precise defect location:

> "Communication: 2/4 — Evidence: …the site URL/name is 'echo nova,' while the band name shown is 'Noise Devoid,' and the contact section says 'reach out to echo nova' (plus 'Contact Noise Deviod' misspelling)."

Source artifact: First Draft Feedback — Scores section

*2- The capped, ordered, executable fix list*

Eleven of twelve documents end with exactly "Top 3 Fix-Next Actions (most important first)," each action self-contained:

> "Action 1: Add playable media: embed at least 1–3 demos/live recordings (Spotify/SoundCloud/MP3) or a rehearsal video if nothing is released yet."

Source artifact: First Draft and Final AlphaTest Feedback documents — Top 3 Fix-Next Actions sections

*3- Same rubric at draft and final, with visible movement*

Final AlphaTest Feedback documents rescore the identical domains; teams that drew 1–2/4 with media/contact gaps at draft reach 3/4 at final with the gap closure named in the evidence ("the embedded Spotify track provides immediate, legitimate proof-of-sound"; "given the band has no studio recordings yet, the included videos function as the primary proof-of-sound").

Source artifact: Final AlphaTest Feedback — Scores sections, compared against the paired drafts

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is either half missing: a score without cited evidence, or evidence without a bounded action plan.

*1- In-corpus partial instance: scores with no fix list*

One draft document carries scores and evidence but no Fix-Next Actions — the student receives a diagnosis with no sized plan for the revision window.

## Reusable Coaching Move

Use the Score–Evidence–Three format for every draft checkpoint:

1. **Score each rubric domain** — same domains you'll use at AlphaTest.
2. **Under each score, cite the artifact:** one quote or concrete observation that earned the score, one that caps it ("what keeps it from 4/4 is…").
3. **Close with exactly three actions, most important first,** each executable without asking you what it means.
4. **At the final, rescore the same domains** and name in the evidence which draft gaps closed.

## Confidence: Medium — 12 documents, uniform structure, five real-client teams and both assessment stages, with draft→final score movement on a stable rubric; single workshop and campus so far, and authorship of the documents (guide vs guide-team) is not recorded in the artifacts.
