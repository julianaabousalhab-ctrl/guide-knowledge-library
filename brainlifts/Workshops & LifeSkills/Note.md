# Node 5.1 — Workshop Planning

## In

- **BL-071:** Publish student-facing pass criteria on day one — ambiguity reads as a motivation problem.
- **BL-103:** Baseline the life skill in week one — progress judgments need a documented start.
- **BL-106:** Decompose before you design — skill → subskill → "I can" behaviors, activity last.
- **BL-110:** Give the work a real client — external stakes produce professional effort.
- **BL-119:** Take the project to the need — source it from the community and keep students in direct contact with the people it serves.

## Out

- **Grit coaching as the response to unclear-criteria failures** — excluded as a positive pattern; the corpus names this the misdiagnosis (a design failure treated as a student failure).
- **Revealing the rubric on assessment day** — retained only as the anti-pattern, with the documented cost.

## Ambiguous

- Pass-criteria design borders on Workshop Assessment. Kept here because the Guide move (translate rubric → publish → reuse the language at launch, midpoint, final) happens at planning time, before any assessment exists.
- BL-119 and BL-110 both externalize the work, at different points: BL-110 gives finished work a real client and real stakes; BL-119 moves discovery itself into the community — the need is found through direct contact before the project is defined, and that contact is maintained. A workshop can do both.

# Node 5.2 — Workshop Assessment

## In

- **BL-090:** Treat mass failure as a design signal — trigger a system review, not individual remediation.
- **BL-104:** "Not passed" is a diagnosis trigger, not a terminal state — close every failure with the component-level gap and the booked response.
- **BL-107:** Run the weekly improvement loop — survey the students, extract three takeaways, change something.
- **BL-127:** Externalize the bar — real experts set and judge the AlphaTest, coached to be rigorous, blind where possible.

## Out

- **N parallel individual remediations after a shared failure** — retained only as the anti-pattern; the corpus records shared failures logged individually with the pattern never triggering a review.
- **Coaching around structural causes** — excluded; where the barrier was product- or design-level, the documented strong response was escalation, not more coaching.

## Ambiguous

- The cohort trigger here vs the individual drift trigger in BL-096 (node 4.2) could have been one pattern. Split by trigger type, with the boundary stated in both files.
- Longitudinal repeated failures of one student across workshops are treated by BL-090's logic (diagnose once), though the evidence for that extension is thinner than the cohort case.
- BL-127 completes the externalization set: BL-110 externalizes purpose and stakes, BL-127 externalizes the standard and its judge — one workshop can carry both. BL-088 is the preparation-side complement (rehearse under the conditions the external judge will impose). BL-090/BL-104 govern what happens after failure; BL-127 governs who decides failure.

# Node 5.3 — Workshop Coaching

## In

- **BL-088:** Rehearse under test conditions — guided success does not transfer on its own.
- **BL-102:** A "Behind" flag is not an intervention — record the barrier and the booked response at the moment of flagging.
- **BL-105:** Score with cited evidence, cap the fixes at three — the draft-checkpoint feedback structure.
- **BL-130:** Split the project 90/10 — students own the work and its fair failures; the adult owns the failure modes students can't control.

## Out

- **Strong guided practice as sufficient preparation** — retained only as the anti-pattern.
- **Letting one final event carry all accountability** — excluded as a positive structure; documented as the failure mode where daily preparation carried none.

## Ambiguous

- Progressive pressure ramps (partner → small group → other group → unfamiliar adults) apply cleanly to performance-based assessments; how much of the ramp transfers to non-performance skills is not settled by the corpus and was left open rather than generalized.
- BL-130's split rests on the fair-failure distinction: under-execution by students stands as a legitimate outcome (the same logic as this node's "one final event" Out-note), while logistics, safety, and external dependencies stay adult-owned so a project can't fail for reasons students never controlled. The in-the-moment discipline of not absorbing student-owned work is BL-123's territory (node 2.3); BL-130 is the project-level ownership architecture.

# Node 5.4 — LifeSkills Development

## In

- **BL-092:** After the workshop ends, install the life skill into the daily routine.

## Out

- **Single-Launch bridging** — deliberately excluded here; bridging one lesson to the next task belongs to Launches (BL-014, BL-017). This node's unit is a session-long installation of one skill across ordinary routines, and BL-092 states that distinction.
- **Started-then-lapsed daily rituals** — retained only as the anti-pattern, including the documented case where the lapse preceded a failed T2P.

## Ambiguous

- One target skill per workshop was the converged practice; whether multiple simultaneous skill installations dilute the effect is not answerable from the current corpus.
