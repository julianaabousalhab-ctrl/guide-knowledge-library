# Run the Weekly Improvement Loop — Survey the Students, Extract Three Takeaways, Commit to One Change

## Pattern

While a workshop is running, the Guide closes a weekly loop on it:

1. **Survey the students each week** — short, workshop-specific questions (effort, excitement, clarity, progress toward the T2P).
2. **Analyze the responses** — in the documented practice, with AI assistance, the conversation linked for review.
3. **Extract exactly three takeaways** — what's high quality, what's low quality, what's surprising.
4. **Commit to one concrete improvement for the upcoming week** — one, not a list: a change specific enough to execute in the next session.

The mechanism: a weekly cadence catches design problems while there are still weeks left to fix them — the same logic as BL-096's timed cycle, applied to the workshop instead of the student. The one-improvement cap (the template's own constraint) forces prioritization and makes the commitment checkable the following week. The linked survey and AI conversation make the reasoning auditable, and a review rating on the entry makes the loop itself accountable.

Related: BL-090 is the event-triggered review (mass failure fires it); this is the cadence-based review that runs regardless — the same pairing as BL-096 (cadence) and BL-090 (event) on the student side. BL-093 documents the student-side mechanism that makes the loop matter: feedback channels die when using them visibly changes nothing. BL-105 is the mirror image (structured feedback *to* students on their work); BL-061's one-priority discipline is the same cap applied in parent communication.

## Evidence

*1- The loop executed at scale, with the cap built into the instrument*

The Guide Reflection datasheet records 108 completed weekly entries across Sessions 1–4 of SY23-24, ~40 named guides, two campuses (Austin, Brownsville), and every level LL–L3 — each entry structured by the template's own prompts: "bullet point the 3 major take-aways" and "what is 1 concrete improvement you will make to the workshop for the upcoming week?" Entries carry links to the survey responses and the AI analysis conversation, and some carry a reviewer rating ("Meets Expectations").

Source artifact:[Workshop Student Feedback & Guide Reflection](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/12mtmLF3sExaKfMjHgXoZGWCQ_Whd_pu2ZXbiT2lDeEU/edit?usp=drive_link)

*2- The improvements are concrete and traceable to the feedback*

> Space constraints limited archery practice → "Implement rotating archery stations with complementary activities… focused on resilience and self-regulation."

> Student apprehension about a vague AlphaTest ("deliver a 20 minute workout session") → the expectation redefined in observable terms: "Introducing yourself as the instructor… Questions, Shout outs, Form corrections… Demonstrating and explaining at the same time. PRACTICE PRACTICE PRACTICE." 

> Uneven group contribution in QUEST → "assign specific group roles to each member," plus a follow-up conversation with the students whose suggestion was unclear.

*3- A documented before/after inside the loop*

One entry records the effect of a prior week's change: "Allowing students to choose their own paintings significantly increased workshop quality… Initially, not allowing students to select paintings limited their engagement" — the loop's previous improvement, evaluated by the loop's next iteration, followed by the next committed change (reframing the AlphaTest as mastery of "a skill that people pay $100 for"). ## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is collection without closure — surveying students and letting the responses end there — and, at system level, letting the loop itself lapse.

*1- The lapse, measured in the same file*

Completed entries decay across the year — 54 in Session 1, 29 in S2, 7 in S3, 15 in S4 — and every SY24-25 sheet in the workbook contains only the blank template: the loop that ran all of 23-24 was not sustained into the next year. This is the ritual-lapse failure BL-092 documents, happening to the improvement ritual itself.

*2- Collection without an action record, in adjacent artifacts*

## Reusable Coaching Move

Close the loop every week the workshop runs:

1. **Ask** — a short weekly pulse tied to the workshop's AlphaTest and experience (effort, excitement, clarity, stuck points).
2. **Analyze** — with AI if useful; link the conversation so the reasoning is auditable.
3. **Name three takeaways** — one high-quality element, one low-quality element, one surprise.
4. **Commit to one change** — written, specific, executable next session. Not three changes; one.
5. **Start next week's entry by checking last week's change** — did it work? (The before/after is the loop's proof it's alive.)

## Confidence: Medium-High for the practice (108 entries, ~40 guides, two campuses, four sessions, with an in-file before/after case); the decay data and empty SY24-25 sheets show sustainment is the unsolved half, and no outcome link (loop adherence → AlphaTest results) exists in the corpus.
