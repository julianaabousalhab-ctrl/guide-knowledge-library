# Externalize the Bar — Real Experts Set and Judge the Test-to-Pass, Coached to Be Rigorous, Blind Where Possible

## Pattern

Across multiple workshops under one lead guide, the assessment standard is deliberately moved outside the guide-student relationship:

1. **External experts judge the T2P** — a professional panel, a chef, domain professionals — not the guide who coached the students.
2. **The judges are coached to rigor** — explicitly instructed to be "rigorous and not lenient," because untrained external judges default to kindness.
3. **Blind where possible** — judging via email against work products, removing presentation charisma and relationship effects.
4. **External reference standards calibrate the bar** — a domain benchmark (junior USTA standards) is consulted to set the pass criterion, then documented.
5. **The experts' credentials are announced to students beforehand** — used deliberately to build anticipation and raise the perceived stakes.

The same externalization appears on the delivery side (vendors teaching the skill), making the guide the orchestrator of expertise rather than the sole source and sole judge.

Related: BL-110 (real client) externalizes *purpose and stakes*; BL-127 externalizes *the standard and its judge* — a workshop can have both. BL-088 (rehearse under test conditions) is the preparation-side complement. BL-090/BL-104 govern what happens after failure; BL-127 governs who decides failure. No contradictions identified.

## Anti-Pattern

The guide as sole source and sole judge: the person who coached the student also decides whether the student passed, with no external reference — so the bar drifts toward the relationship, and students read the pass as the guide's opinion rather than the world's standard. Sub-failure: recruiting external judges but not briefing them, producing lenient scores that undercut the whole mechanism.

## Reusable Coaching Move

1. **Recruit the judge early** — a domain professional, confirmed weeks before the T2P.
2. **Brief for rigor** — tell judges explicitly that leniency is the failure mode; give them the pass criteria.
3. **Blind what you can** — email-based judging of work products where the format allows.
4. **Calibrate against the world** — anchor the pass bar to an external domain standard and document it.
5. **Spend the credentials** — introduce the judges' backgrounds to students before the deadline, as stakes.

## Confidence: Medium — five instances with all five mechanism components explicitly documented, but concentrated under a single lead guide (three guides, one campus). A batch-5 observation (Mary Schroll consulting 15 search-and-rescue experts for workshop design, different campus and lead) corroborates the direction from a separate corpus but was itself single-instance. Raises to Medium-High with a second lead guide independently running expert-judged Alphatest.
