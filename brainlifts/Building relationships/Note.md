# Node 1.1 — Building Trust

## In

- **BL-018:** Validate the student's perspective before challenging it.
- **BL-019:** Adjust the Guide's approach when the student signals a mismatch.
- **BL-020:** Establish and honor recording boundaries.
- **BL-021:** Make the purpose and decision rules explicit before asking for buy-in.
- **BL-091:** Induct new joiners into owning the community — don't hand them a finished system.
- **BL-115:** Run the guide transition as a relationship handoff — rapport before coaching, assets before agenda.
- **BL-124:** Trust is working capital — once built, the coached next move is to spend it on higher standards.

## Out

- **Protect disclosure or confidentiality beyond recording** — not included because the transcript evidence was not strong or consistent enough to support a separate pattern.
- **Repair relational ruptures explicitly** — not included because the observed behaviors were already covered by validation, adaptive coaching, and repair pathways.
- **Make the agenda transparent and co-owned** — not included separately because the strongest evidence concerned explaining purpose and decision rules rather than jointly setting the full agenda.

## Ambiguous

- **Recording boundaries** was the least certain pattern in this node. Some examples involved simple procedural consent rather than deeper coaching behavior.
- It was retained because the corpus included direct permission, off-record options, and immediate honoring of a pause request.
- Evidence was limited to explicit transcript language. No assumptions were made about privacy policy, access, or confidentiality beyond what the meetings stated.
- BL-091 and BL-115 are a mirror pair: BL-091 covers the student new to the community; BL-115 covers the adult new to the student. Both retained because trigger and actor differ.
- BL-115's "walk the inherited record with the student" borders 1.2's use of student knowledge (BL-023); separated because BL-115 governs transferring the relationship, BL-023 governs using knowledge inside an established one. BL-115's executed evidence is one campus/era (see its Confidence section).
- BL-124 sits at this node's exit: BL-018–BL-021 and BL-115 build and transfer trust; BL-124 governs the moment trust exists — converting it into tighter accountability and higher expectations. The accountability mechanics themselves (agreements, repair) stay in 2.3; BL-124 covers the relational timing of the shift, not the mechanics.

# Node 1.2 — Getting to Know Students

## In

- **BL-022:** Explore the student's interests until they reveal a usable coaching lever.
- **BL-023:** Use student knowledge to personalize later coaching.
- **BL-117:** Show up in the student's arena — attend their outside-school moments as deliberate relationship investment.
- **BL-129:** Give the student more than one anchor — interests, strategies, and exploitable seams travel to every adult who shares them.

## Out

- **Build every goal around a student interest** — not included because interests should inform coaching only when the connection is authentic.
- **General rapport-building conversation** — not included because friendly conversation alone does not demonstrate that the Guide learned something usable.
- **Remember personal facts about the student** — not included as a separate pattern because remembering matters only when it changes later coaching.

## Ambiguous

- Some interest conversations created strong rapport but did not produce a coaching application.
- These were treated as anti-patterns or incomplete examples rather than evidence of the pattern.
- A coaching lever required a visible connection to a resource, strategy, opportunity, responsibility, or next step.
- BL-117 begins where BL-022 ends: the interest is already known; the move is physical presence in the student's outside-school context, invitation-gated and family-coordinated. It is not coaching personalization (BL-023) and not a reward (BL-066).
- BL-129 distributes what BL-022 and BL-117 generate: student knowledge as team property rather than the assigned guide's private capital. It is the always-on version of BL-115's one-time transition handoff, and covers interests/strategies/context where BL-095 covers consequence consistency. BL-098's documented protocols are one instrument its strategy-transfer can use. This candidate upgrades a batch-6 first-pass reject ("communicate strategies to cover guides"), previously held for single-lead concentration — a second corpus resolved that.

# Node 1.3 — Student Voice

## In

- **BL-024:** Ask for the student's solution before supplying one.
- **BL-025:** Preserve student agency when advocating or escalating.
- **BL-093:** Close the loop on student voice — act, attribute, or explain.

## Out

- **Make more space for student thinking** — not included separately because it overlapped with asking for the student's solution and probing reasoning.
- **Limit Guide monologues** — treated as a quality indicator or anti-pattern, not a distinct coaching pattern.
- **Always require the student to advocate alone** — not included because some situations legitimately require adult authority or escalation.

## Ambiguous

- Guide suggestions were not automatically treated as reducing student voice.
- The deciding factor was sequence: whether the student had a meaningful opportunity to think or speak first.
- Adult escalation was considered agency-preserving when the student remained informed or involved and the Guide added only the support necessary.
- BL-093 governs what happens after voice is heard (visible action, attribution, or explained constraints; delegated decisions are never revoked post hoc). It does not overlap 6.2's in-meeting facilitation patterns (BL-055/056) or BL-024's think-first sequence.

# Node 1.4 — Celebration & Recognition

## In

- **BL-026:** Recognize the specific behavior or strategy behind progress.
- **BL-027:** Connect recognition to growth over time.

## Out

- **General praise and encouragement** — not included because it does not show the student what produced the result.
- **Celebrate high scores, XP, or completed checks** — not included as a separate pattern unless the Guide connected the outcome to controllable behavior or prior growth.
- **Praise identity or talent** — excluded as evidence because it can reinforce a fixed-trait interpretation.

## Ambiguous

- Some examples contained both specific strategy recognition and growth-over-time recognition.
- They were assigned according to the primary coaching move: naming what produced the result; or comparing the current state with an earlier baseline.
- The same meeting could support both patterns only when it contained clearly distinct statements.
