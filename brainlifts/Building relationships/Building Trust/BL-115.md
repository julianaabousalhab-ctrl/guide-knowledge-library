# Run the Guide Transition as a Relationship Handoff — Rapport Before Coaching, Assets Before Agenda

## Pattern

When a student changes guides, the coaching relationship restarts from zero unless the transition is run deliberately. Students anticipate the change with anxiety, and first meetings run cold produce shrugs where the previous guide had depth. Guides who transition well converge on the same sequence — treating the handoff as a relationship transfer, not a records transfer:

1. **Informal contact before the first formal meeting** — a lunch or casual connection first, so the first Limitless Meeting isn't also the first real conversation.
2. **First meeting on the student's territory** — the explicit objective is a trust baseline, spent on the student's passions and projects, with coaching depth deliberately deferred; a temporarily "surface level" relationship is accepted as the correct starting state, not rushed past.
3. **Walk the inherited record with the student** — review the profile, report, or prior-guide context together, so the student knows what the new guide knows and continuity is visible rather than assumed.
4. **Take the handoff of what already works** — the outgoing guide passes the relationship assets explicitly: the trust anchors, working rituals, norms, and inside jokes, so the new guide doesn't unknowingly retire them.

This is the mirror of BL-091: there the student is new to the community; here the adult is new to the student. Both share the mechanism that belonging and trust are rebuilt by deliberate structure, not by time passing.

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is treating the transition as an administrative fact — new name on the roster, records forwarded — and coaching from meeting one as if the relationship transferred with the paperwork.

*The reset, observed from both sides*

The student side, when no pre-work preceded the first meeting: "This was my first official meeting with Zion, but he did not volunteer much when I asked him questions. I was met with a lot of 'I don't knows' and shrugs. ... it might take time for him to build trust with his new guide and open up." The anticipatory cost appears even before the change: "Eddie is stuck in fear. He is nervous about getting a new guide."

The year-scale cost when transitions kept resetting the environment: "When guide changes and new kids joined mid year, the class culture Cameron needed to feel safe kept resetting, even with weekly one on ones, session lunches, and academic goal setting all in place. ... without a stable culture to lean on, school never fully won out."

## Reusable Coaching Move

Use the Contact → Territory → Record → Assets process for every guide change.

### 1. Contact before the meeting

Get one informal touchpoint (lunch, a shared activity, drop-off conversations) on the calendar before the first Limitless Meeting. The first formal meeting should be the second conversation, not the first.

### 2. Spend the first meeting on their territory

State the objective honestly — building the baseline — and spend the time on the student's passions and current projects. Do not force coaching depth; name the temporary shallowness as expected and fine. Anticipate the dip: shrugs and "I don't know" are transition signatures, not verdicts on the student.

### 3. Review the record with the student

Go through the inherited profile or report together so the student knows what traveled with them — and correct it with them where it's stale (their feedback protocol per BL-098, their motivators per BL-066).

### 4. Take the assets, not just the data

Debrief the outgoing guide for what the file won't say: the working rituals, the trust anchors, the phrases and jokes that carry weight. Keep those running until the student is ready to renegotiate them — retiring them unknowingly is how transitions reset progress.

The coaching standard is not:

> "The new guide received the student's records and scheduled their first meeting."

The standard is:

> "By the first formal meeting the student had already met the new guide informally, the meeting ran on the student's territory, the inherited record was walked through together — and the rituals that worked last session are still running this one."

## Confidence: Medium — three guides executed the sequence with observed anti-patterns on both sides, but all executed arcs are one campus/era (possible shared-coaching confound), the cross-campus leg is design-only, and success evidence is immediate guide self-report.
