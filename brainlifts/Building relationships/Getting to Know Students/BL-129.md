# Give the Student More Than One Anchor — Interests, Strategies, and Exploitable Seams Travel to Every Adult Who Shares Them

## Pattern

Two lead guides at different campuses coach the same rule: knowledge about a student is team property, not the assigned guide's private capital. Three kinds of knowledge travel, each with its own payoff:

1. **Interests** — shared so *other* adults can build independent rapport anchors ("he loves scary stuff" → routed to the guide who grew up on Goosebumps; a hockey obsession → a visiting guide who knew a trading-card store). The stated goal is resilience: the student's day should survive their primary guide's absence.
2. **Working strategies** — individualized regulation and coaching strategies are explicitly transferred to fill-in guides so coverage days stay consistent.
3. **Exploitable seams** — history and family context that a student uses to play adults against each other (the "mom said no, ask dad" move, tech-restriction history) is briefed to every new adult before the student finds the gap.

## Evidence

*1- The mechanism stated in full, with the resilience rationale (first lead)*

> "if you hear that a kid's interested in something, tell us... he is going to be with you more than the rest of us. However, the idea is for him to love all of us because we want him to feel safe with everybody and loved by everybody at Alpha Palm Beach as a whole. And like, so when you share like, 'Hey, he loves scary stuff.' You know who loves scary stuff? [Guide]."

> "...finding roots outside of just you. So it's not just like, 'Hey, if [Guide] is gone that day, is it going to be the end of the world?' ... he's built confidence anchors in her..."

Matching action item: "Share [Student]'s known interests (scary YouTube characters, animatronics, Mark Rober, building) with the broader team — specifically flag the scary/horror interest to [Guide] — so other guides can build independent rapport anchors with him."

Source: snapshots transcript, Allie Bradian ↔ Erin Colucci 1:1, 2026-05-05. Matching action item (2026-05-07)

*2- Strategy transfer to cover guides (same lead, second guide)*

> "[Guide A] has developed effective individualized strategies (e.g., giving [Student] space to self-regulate) that aren't yet consistently communicated to [Guide B] or other fill-in guides. Consider proactively sharing student-specific strategies so coverage days remain consistent."

Source: feedback Allie Bradian → Sean Mckenna, 2026-05-07.

*3- Seam briefing for a new guide (second lead, second campus)*

In a Guide's first-week 1:1, the lead pre-briefs student's exploit pattern and its context:

> "it was the epitome of Mom said, no, I'm gonna go ask dad. He would do it constantly, where he'd ask [Guide] something, [Guide] would say, no, he'd come ask me, or vice versa."

followed by the student's technology-restriction history and family context ("his mom does not allow him to have any technology in his life at all whatsoever"), his motivational profile (carrot vs. stick), and live seam-checks between the adults ("he asked me yesterday, can I bring my baseball back to school? He didn't ask you that?"). 

Source: snapshots record, Samantha DePalo ↔ Chloe Horn 1:1, 2026-05-01.

*4- Resource-matching payoff, cross-adult*

> "...using each other as resources... we can have ideas and then someone else might have the resource to capitalize on said idea..."

Source: same transcript as Evidence 1 (hockey interest → guide visitor → trading-card store).

## Anti-Pattern

Knowledge siloed in the assigned guide: the student has exactly one anchor adult (absence = lost day), cover guides improvise against strategies they've never heard of, and the student finds and works the seams between adults who don't compare notes.

## Reusable Coaching Move

1. **Interest heard → interest shared** — any adult who learns a student interest posts it to the team, tagged to whoever shares that interest.
2. **Strategy works → strategy documented for coverage** — the individualized move is written where fill-in guides will see it, not carried in the guide's head.
3. **New adult → seam briefing** — before a new guide meets the group, brief them on each student's history, family context, and known plays against adult inconsistency.
4. **Verify the seams live** — adults spot-check with each other ("did he ask you that too?") so students experience one consistent adult system.

## Confidence: Medium-High — two lead guides at two campuses, four instances covering three distinct knowledge types, with the mechanism and its rationale stated verbatim by one lead and enacted in a first-week briefing by the other. Capped below High pending a third campus or student-level outcome data (e.g., coverage-day stability).
