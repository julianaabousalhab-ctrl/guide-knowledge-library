# Show Up in the Student's Arena — Attend Their Outside-School Moments as Deliberate Relationship Investment

## Pattern

When a 1:1 lunch surfaces the student's real arena — the soccer team, the recital, the horse show — the strongest converged next step across guides is not to theme coaching around it but to physically show up in it. The plan-of-action entries share four features:

1. **Invitation first** — attendance is offered, not imposed ("ask him if he'd like me to come to see him play soccer and go if he says yes").
2. **Family-coordinated logistics** — the guide reaches the parents to get the schedule, making the commitment concrete rather than sentimental ("connect with Vance/Brandi to learn when her horse shows are so I can attend").
3. **Escalation from spectator to participant when invited** — the deepest version has the student teach the guide their craft ("Our ultimate goal is for Anna to teach me her current ballet routine so that I could be her official dance partner at Alpha").
4. **Presence paired with continuity** — the visit anchors an ongoing thread ("I'll ask Miles on a weekly basis about his progress to show that I believe in him"), so it reads as belief, not a one-off appearance.

The mechanism: interest knowledge (BL-022) tells the student you listened; showing up in their arena tells them you *invested* — on their turf, where they are the expert and school metrics don't exist.

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is letting the relationship narrow to academic monitoring — the guide present in the student's metrics but absent from the student's world — documented in a guide's own realization at one of these lunches.

*The relationship reduced to check-chart oversight, named by the guide*

> "Often, my interactions with [Student] are clouded by my focus on her academic progress (specifically the fact that she is very behind on checks). Having lunch with [Student] put into perspective how short a time I have left with her."

The interest inventory existed; what was missing until the lunch recalibrated it was any investment in Student as a person outside the academic ledger — the gap this pattern's presence-moves are designed to close.

## Reusable Coaching Move

Use the Discover → Offer → Coordinate → Continue process.

### 1. Discover the arena

From the 1:1 lunch (or profile), identify the one outside-school context where the student performs, competes, or creates — the place they are the expert.

### 2. Offer, don't announce

Ask whether they'd like you there, and go only on a yes. The invitation is part of the move: it hands the student control of the boundary between school and their world.

### 3. Coordinate through the family

Get the actual schedule from parents and commit to a date. A named event on the calendar separates investment from sentiment — and doubles as a parent-partnership touchpoint.

### 4. Continue after you show up

Follow the visit with a standing thread (weekly progress asks, learning a piece of their craft if invited). One appearance signals interest; the thread signals belief.

The coaching standard is not:

> "The Guide knows what the student does outside school and references it warmly."

The standard is:

> "The Guide has been in the stands, at the recital, or on the sideline — invited, scheduled through the family, and followed by a thread the student can feel week after week."
