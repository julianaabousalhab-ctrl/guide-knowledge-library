# For the Stalled Student, Structure the Choice — Assigned Core Plus Student-Chosen Remainder

## Pattern

For students stalled on open-ended work (check charts in every observed instance), three lead guides coached the same structural correction: open choice is the stall condition, so replace it with a structured schedule — without removing choice entirely. The precise mechanism, stated once in full:

- **The guide pre-selects a core** (2 of 5 checks) and **the student chooses the remainder** (3 of 5) — "to build autonomy while maintaining momentum."
- Variants: a focused multi-week schedule "prioritizing single assigned tasks" for a student who loops on task-switching; a structured completion plan paired with individual motivational models.

The claim is narrower than "add structure": it is that *choice itself is the load* for these students, and the fix is a designed mix — assigned enough to guarantee motion, chosen enough to preserve ownership.

Related: BL-072/BL-085 (same node) manage momentum through sequencing and progress visibility while keeping the task set fixed; BL-128 redesigns the *option set*. Consistent with the node's Out-note (workload is not lowered — only choice structure changes). BL-033 (co-created accountability agreement) is the agreement-layer complement; BL-082 (students author the model) pulls the other direction on ownership — flagged as a tension to reconcile in review, not a contradiction: BL-082 governs incentive design, BL-128 governs task selection under stall conditions.

## Anti-Pattern

Leaving the stalled student in full-menu mode: the check chart as an open field of equally weighted options, where the student's energy goes into choosing (or avoiding choosing) instead of completing — then reading the stall as a motivation problem.

## Reusable Coaching Move

1. **Spot the stall type** — progress flat while the option set is wide; the student circles, task-switches, or avoids starting.
2. **Assign the core** — the guide pre-selects the subset that guarantees weekly motion (including any strategically important items).
3. **Hand back the remainder** — the student chooses the rest; the choice is real but bounded.
4. **Put it on a clock** — the schedule spans named weeks with a review checkpoint ("on pace for 2 bingos?").
5. **Fade** — as momentum returns, widen the chosen share before removing the structure.
