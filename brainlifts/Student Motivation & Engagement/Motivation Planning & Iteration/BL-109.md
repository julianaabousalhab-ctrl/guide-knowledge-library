# Refresh Every Student's Motivational Plan on a Staff-Day Cadence — Academics Narrative In, Revised Plan Out, Version Saved

## Pattern

The motivational plan is a living document revised on a fixed cadence — at staff days — through a three-part record per student:

1. **The academics narrative comes in:** a data-grounded, per-subject account from the academics side of what actually happened ("losing 20% of her math time to idling and peer conversations"; "averaged only 5 minutes and 7 points per day in Language, far below the 25-minute target"), often with the structural response taken ("has been moved to AlphaMath for better, video-based instruction").
2. **The Guide writes the revised plan against it:** the new version responds to the named problems with adjusted levers — and records *why* the previous version is being changed: "[Student] has not been motivated by either of the plans she has created for the last two weeks… her suggestion this week: if she does not complete her epic day… she will receive a Canva video where an Axolotl is at risk of being eaten by a shark."
3. **The version is saved, dated, and attributed:** plan histories carry v1/v2 with timestamps, author names, and archive status — so any adult can see what was tried, when it changed, and what the current plan is.

The mechanism: motivational plans decay silently — a plan that worked in February is stale by April, and without a forced cadence plus the academics-side narrative, the Guide is revising from memory against drifting data. The paired record (their data story + your plan revision) is what makes each version a decision rather than a rewrite. Student co-authorship of the revision ([Student]'s own suggested consequence) applies BL-082 inside the refresh.

Related: this is the 1:1-plan sibling of BL-107's weekly workshop loop — same architecture (evidence in, one committed revision out, record kept) at a different cadence and grain. BL-069 and BL-080 govern *what* the revision should do (move the bar, design the exit); this governs *when and how* revisions happen and get recorded. BL-042's revisit-with-evidence is the same move done conversationally in 1:1s.

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is the skipped refresh: the cadence fires, and the "revision" is a paste or a blank.

*1- In-corpus instance*

[Student]'s plan history shows v1, quality 0, whose text is a verbatim copy of his April staff-day narrative — a character description ("confident and well-rounded… embraces challenges with grit") standing in the plan slot, with no lever, bar, or reward: the refresh recorded as done without a plan being made.

## Reusable Coaching Move

At every staff-day cadence, per student:

1. **Read their data story first** — the academics narrative (or your own data pull): what actually happened, in which subjects, and why.
2. **Judge the current plan against it:** keep, tweak, or rework — and if the plan hasn't moved behavior (two flat weeks is the observed trigger), rework, ideally with the student supplying the new mechanism (BL-082).
3. **Write the new version so it opens with the why:** what the last version didn't do, what changes, what would count as it working.
4. **Save it as a version** — dated, attributed — never overwrite. The history is the record of what this student responds to (BL-098's logic at plan level).
5. **Refuse the paste:** a description of the student is not a plan. If it has no lever, bar, or reward, the refresh isn't done.

## Confidence: Medium — a consistent three-part record across 5 sampled students and at least two named guides on two staff-day cycles, with a within-corpus anti-instance; drawn from one platform reviewed in a screenshot walkthrough, with no outcome link from refresh quality to student response yet.
