# Write the Quality Condition Into the Earning Rule — Make Gaming the Incentive Unprofitable

## Pattern

When an incentive pays out on a quantity (XP, points, completions), students who want the reward find the fastest route to the number — speeding through lessons, taking below-level tests, skipping instruction. The strong Guide response is structural, not conversational: the quality condition is written into the earning rule itself, so the gamed route stops paying.

Observed forms of the quality gate:

1. **Accuracy floors:** "he can earn coding time during CoreSkills once he reaches 180 XP *with an 80% average accuracy* across his apps — and we will raise the threshold once he demonstrates consistent mastery."
2. **Time-on-task conditions:** a test reward that requires improving the score *and* "takes longer than 45 min on a test" — paying for engagement, not speed.
3. **Visible-work requirements:** math rewards gated on "a full written problem-solving page per day on paper… showing his work clearly," not on points.
4. **Growth-gated unlocks:** a reward tracker that "will also only be able to be unlocked if 2x MAP scores and new daily minimums are hit weekly" across all subjects — the incentive pays on learning outcomes, not raw output.

The mechanism: coaching students about what XP means (BL-084) loses to an incentive that still pays for farming. Changing the earning rule changes the payoff structure — the reward and the learning stop being separable, and the rising threshold ("we will raise it once he demonstrates mastery") converts the gate into a progression.

Related: BL-084 is the coaching conversation about XP-as-progress; this is the incentive-design enforcement of the same principle. BL-066 governs choosing the reward; this governs the rule that earns it. The rising threshold applies BL-069's raise-after-success inside the rule.

## Evidence

*1- The gaming documented first, in the academics narratives*

The April staff-day narratives record quantity-incentive gaming precisely: one student "wasted 80% of his Reading time by speeding through lessons in under 2 minutes and taking below-grade-level tests for easy points"; another "skips instructional examples and rushes past error explanations in Math… keeping his accuracy below the level needed to move from lessons to tests."

Source artifact: MAP Goals Platform

*2- The re-designed earning rules, across students and guides*

The same students' revised plans embed the quality gates: 180 XP *at 80% accuracy* with a rising threshold (Helio); a full written problem-solving page per day "showing his work clearly" with the reward chosen together at completion (Duke); a Pokemon tracker unlocked only by weekly 2x MAP scores plus new daily minimums in four subjects (Duke, April plan).

Source artifact: MAP Goals Platform

*3- Quality conditions on test rewards*

A third student's incentive structure pays "$20 toward hunting/cooking gear" only when he "improves his score on a test (out of all previous scores) *and* takes longer than 45 min on a test" — an explicit anti-rushing condition inside the reward table.

Source artifact: MAP Goals Platform

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is paying on the number alone — an incentive attached to XP or completion counts with no accuracy, time, or work-quality condition — which the corpus shows students optimizing exactly as written.

*1- The documented cost of quantity-only incentives*

The February plans rewarded output ("as Iker independently completes work, he will earn points toward incentives"); by April, the academics narratives document the speed-gaming those rules permitted (sub-2-minute lessons, below-grade-level tests for easy points, 20–30% of time wasted) — the before state of the same students whose rules were then re-written.

Source artifact: MAP Goals Platform

*2- The library's parallel*

BL-084's anti-pattern records the conversational version: an incentive negotiated around a single XP total with no learning-quality protection.

Source artifact: cross-reference BL-084, Anti-Pattern

## Reusable Coaching Move

Before launching any incentive, run the Gaming Test on the earning rule:

1. **Ask: "What is the fastest way to earn this that involves no learning?"** — speeding, easy tests, abandoned quality. If that route pays, the rule is unfinished.
2. **Add the cheapest condition that closes it:** an accuracy floor, a minimum time-on-task, visible work, or a growth-gated unlock — matched to the gaming route you found.
3. **Write the rise into the rule:** "threshold increases once you show consistent mastery" — so the gate becomes a ladder (BL-069).
4. **Keep the reward value-matched (BL-066)** — the gate constrains *how* it's earned, never *whether* it's worth earning.

## Confidence: Medium — 3+ students with re-designed rules across at least two named guides, with the before-state (gaming under quantity rules) documented in the same corpus; drawn from one platform's records reviewed in a screenshot walkthrough, and no post-change outcome data yet.
