# Node 3.1 — Motivational Models

## In

- **BL-066:** Match the reward to the student's demonstrated value system, not a generic prize.
- **BL-067:** Turn a distracting friendship into a joint contingency.
- **BL-082:** Let students author the model — co-design, vote, or self-build the incentive system.

## Out

- **Generic prizes as a default lever** — retained only as the anti-pattern; a flat response to a generic incentive was treated as evidence of reward mismatch, not of an unmotivatable student.
- **Seat changes / separation as a motivational solution** — the corpus shows separation as a holding action that stops active distraction; the durable gains came from the joint contingency, so separation was not written up as a standalone pattern.
- **Labeling students "unmotivated"** — excluded as evidence anywhere in the category; every flat response in the corpus had a structural explanation (wrong reward, missing authorship, wrong horizon).

## Ambiguous

- Authorship (BL-082) and 1:1 co-design of a single incentive (BL-083, node 3.2) describe the same principle at different scales. Resolved with Related notes in both rather than merging, because the evidence corpora and the Guide-facing move differ.
- What a student proposes when co-designing doubles as diagnostic information about their value system; handled by cross-referencing BL-082 → BL-066 instead of creating a separate diagnostic pattern.

# Node 3.2 — Rewards & Incentives

## In

- **BL-068:** Make progress physically manipulable and keep the incentive in sight.
- **BL-081:** Build the reward ladder from earned status, autonomy, and responsibility — not prizes.
- **BL-083:** Co-design incentives around meaningful progress.
- **BL-108:** Write the quality condition into the earning rule — make gaming structurally impossible.
- **BL-112:** Point multipliers steer effort — they don't create it; use them for salience, not as the engine.

## Out

- **Instant material prizes as the level-wide default** — excluded as a positive pattern; two campuses' own model rationales document the damage of prize-based regimes (recorded in BL-081).
- **Rewards that generate unstructured peer time during work** — treated as an anti-pattern (recorded under BL-067).

## Ambiguous

- The category holds an apparent tension: value-matched material rewards work (BL-066, BL-068) while prize ladders fail (BL-081). Resolved with scope notes in BL-066/BL-081: temporary individual models against a diagnosed gap vs durable level-wide systems. Both remain in, bounded.
- Visibility as a mechanic (BL-068) borders on the reward-dependence failure state described in BL-080; handled with a scope note tying visible trackers to models with a designed exit.

## In

## Out

# Node 3.4 — Maintaining Momentum

## In

- **BL-072:** Front-load a quick win and keep the goal horizon inside the student's motivational window.
- **BL-085:** Make a distant goal feel reachable through visible micro-progress.
- **BL-128:** For the stalled student, structure the choice — assigned core plus student-chosen remainder.

## Out

- **Lowering the workload to protect momentum** — excluded; both patterns explicitly keep the full workload and change only ordering, chunking, or visibility.

## Ambiguous

- The two patterns are the same chunking mechanism at two time scales. Kept separate with Boundary notes in both: BL-072 governs within-block sequencing; BL-085 governs long-horizon goals that should not shrink.
- BL-072/BL-085 manage momentum through sequencing and progress visibility while keeping the task set fixed; BL-128 redesigns the option set itself. It honors this node's Out-note — workload is never lowered, only the choice structure changes. BL-082 pulls the other direction on ownership; flagged as a tension to reconcile in review, not a contradiction: BL-082 governs incentive design, BL-128 governs task selection under stall conditions.

# Node 3.5 — Motivation Planning & Iteration

## In

- **BL-069:** Move the motivational bar in both directions on a weekly cadence.
- **BL-080:** Treat the model as scaffolding — launch it against a diagnosed gap and design its retirement.
- **BL-109:** Refresh every student's motivational plan on a staff-day cadence.
- **BL-121:** A relationship is not a motivational system — install a named structure for every flagged student, and enforce it without fear.

## Out

- **"Continue with the model" as a standing decision** — excluded as a positive pattern; the corpus documents decay in both directions when the bar stays static.
- **Incentives justified by the general goodness of motivation** — excluded; models without a gap statement have no off-ramp and no success test.

## Ambiguous

- The raise-vs-shrink signal is behavioral (ease/boredom vs fading effort/frustration), not numeric; examples were included only where the Guide's observed adjustment matched the stated signal.
- BL-080 and BL-069 govern how a motivational model is launched, adjusted, and retired once it exists; BL-121 governs the prior failure mode — no system existing at all because the relationship was treated as the intervention. Consistent with BL-036 (accountability paired with repair) and BL-095 (consequences calibrated across guides).
