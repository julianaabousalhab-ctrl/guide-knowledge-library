# Coach the Guide's Vocabulary — Plain Words In, Adult Labels Out, Student-Generated Language Preferred

## Pattern

Three lead guides, coaching three different guides within five weeks, converged on the same rule: what a guide says to a student is a coached, auditable behavior. The rule has three parts:

1. **Plain, direct coaching language** — observable and encouraging ("I believe in you," "you can do this"), never clinical or psychological framing, even when the framing is accurate. The bar is not accuracy; it is what the word does when the student re-tells it at home.
2. **No adult-owned labels** — the guide does not assign feelings, identity, or difficulty judgments ("easy") to the student's experience.
3. **Student-generated vocabulary** — where a word matters, the guide prompts the student to name their own experience of success or challenge rather than naming it for them.

A fourth, age-specific corollary: with younger students, vocabulary is previewed before launches and misfires are debriefed.

Related: BL-062 covers the parent-facing analogue (observable behavior, not character); BL-003/BL-099 cover growth-mindset language inside launches; BL-039/BL-040 cover metacognitive language. None governs guide word-choice discipline in student-facing coaching conversations — this fills that gap. No contradictions identified.

## Evidence

*1- Clinical term escalates to a parent complaint; plain-language rule stated*

> "The guide introduced the concept of 'imposter syndrome' with Blair with good intentions, but the psychological framing was misconstrued and escalated to a parent complaint. The suggestion is to stick to clear, direct coaching language (e.g., 'I believe in you,' 'you can do this') rather than clinical or psychological terminology, especially given parent backgrounds and the way students may re-communicate things at home."

Source: feedback Samantha DePalo → Bryce Derry, 2026-04-05. Reinforced by coaching point (same guide/lead, 2026-04-05): "Avoid over-intellectualizing during student and parent conversations. Remember your role is to mentor with high standards and high support, not address underlying causes."

*2- Vocabulary audit as an explicit action item; student-generated language as the replacement*

> "Audit vocabulary used with students — specifically remove or reframe use of the word 'easy' and replace with prompts that invite students to describe their own experience of success or challenge."

Source: action item, Ashley Storms → Jessica Criss, 2026-05-04; with coaching points "Avoiding Adult Assignment of Feelings and Identity" (2026-04-03) and "Letting Students Generate Their Own Vocabulary" (2026-05-04), and the agenda follow-up "is Jessica successfully prompting Octavia to generate her own vocabulary around success rather than assigning it?" (2026-05-04).

*3- Age-calibration corollary: preview vocabulary for younger students*

> "[Guide] acknowledged the challenge of calibrating her language for [Student]'s literal, six-year-old comprehension level — using words like 'takeaway' that didn't land as intended... Encourage [Guide] to preview vocabulary choices before launches with younger students and to debrief those moments when they happen."

Source: feedback Allie Bradian → Erin Colucci, 2026-05-07.

## Anti-Pattern

The guide reaches for sophisticated framing — clinical terms, identity language, adult difficulty judgments — to show insight or empathy. Downstream, the word is misconstrued as it travels home (Evidence 1), or the student absorbs the adult's label instead of building their own narration of success and struggle (Evidence 2).

## Reusable Coaching Move

1. **Audit** — review your recorded meetings for adult-owned words: clinical terms, identity assignments, difficulty judgments ("easy," "hard for you").
2. **Replace** — swap each for either an observable-plain statement or a prompt that hands the naming to the student ("How did that feel when it clicked?" not "That was easy, right?").
3. **Preview** — before launches with younger students, check key words against the youngest listener's literal comprehension.
4. **Debrief** — when a word misfires, debrief the moment rather than letting it pass.

The standard is not "the Guide speaks warmly and knowledgeably." The standard is: "the words students use about themselves are their own, and nothing the Guide says needs translation when it reaches the dinner table."
