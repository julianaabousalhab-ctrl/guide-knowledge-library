# Route the Diagnosed Barrier to a Named Owner — DRI Coordination With an Evidence Package, Not Solo Heroics

## Pattern

When a student's barrier is diagnosed as structural — wrong placement, app misfit, curriculum imbalance, content holes — the coached move across four lead guides is not for the guide to compensate harder inside the classroom. It is routed advocacy:

1. **Name the owner** — the specific DRI or specialist who controls the structure (math DRI, science DRI, academics team, placement owner), by name where possible.
2. **Make a structural ask, not a complaint** — "targeted hole-filling strategies," "a structural fix," "advocate for different placement," "adjustments before MAP" — the ask names what should change in the system.
3. **Attach the guide's evidence package** — behavioral observations the specialist cannot see from data alone travel with the request.
4. **Keep the loop open** — the coordination is a standing agenda item until the structural change lands (the tracking half is BL-043).

Related: BL-041 supplies the upstream diagnosis (strategy vs. fit vs. system); BL-043 supplies the downstream tracking (commitments to closure — its own notes say one message to a system contact is not closure). BL-126 is the move between them: who gets the problem and what travels with it. BL-102 is the workshop-local analogue (barrier + booked response). No contradictions identified.

## Evidence

*1- Named DRIs, structural asks, standing follow-up (first lead)*

> "Continue coordinating with the math DRI on targeted hole-filling strategies for [Student A] and [Student B] to help break their doom loops." / "Maintain ongoing communication with the science DRI regarding the XP-to-slide imbalance in science to find a structural fix for disengaged students like [Student C]."

Source: action items, Allie Bradian → Sean Mckenna, 2026-05-07; both carried forward as agenda items the same week.

*2- Same lead, second guide, named specialist with a deadline-bound ask*

> "Coordinate with [Staff] (math DRI) to finalize and implement more precise academic adjustments for [Student] before MAP testing."

Source: action item, Allie Bradian → Scott Dangerfield, 2026-05-07.

*3- Second lead: placement advocacy with the diagnosis rule stated*

> "Figure out why [Student] has Math Cakes & advocate for different placement - if it's gaps - he has passed and is ready for a new app"

Source: agenda item, Jennifer Maddox → Sanura Njaka, 2026-05-18.

*4- Third lead: the evidence package made explicit*

> "Write up brief bullet-point guide observations for [Student A] covering avoidance behaviors, dislike of being wrong, and friction-related task incompletion, to be sent alongside the academic deep dive to [Student B]."

Source: action item, Samantha DePalo → Jessica Staggs, 2026-05-11.

*5- Fourth lead: cross-team context compilation before a decision point*

> "Collaborate with [Student] and academics team to compile additional context on Fast Math and Reading challenges for SM before MY report meeting with parents"

Source: action item, Matt Shenker → Christian Gordon, 2026-03-17.

## Anti-Pattern

Solo heroics: the guide absorbs a structural problem as a coaching workload — re-motivating a misplaced student, hand-scaffolding around an app misfit — while the structure that produces the struggle stays untouched and the specialist who owns it never hears about it.

## Reusable Coaching Move

When a diagnosis (per BL-041) lands on system or instructional fit rather than student strategy:

1. **Owner** — identify who controls that structure; use their name in the action item.
2. **Ask** — phrase the request as the structural change needed, with a deadline anchor where one exists (MAP, report meetings).
3. **Evidence** — attach short, observable guide notes the data doesn't show (avoidance behaviors, emotional responses, friction points).
4. **Loop** — keep it on the 1:1 agenda until the change is implemented, then verify the student-level effect.

## Confidence: Medium-High — four lead guides, five guides, same routing structure within nine weeks; the evidence-package and diagnosis-rule components are each explicit in only one instance.
