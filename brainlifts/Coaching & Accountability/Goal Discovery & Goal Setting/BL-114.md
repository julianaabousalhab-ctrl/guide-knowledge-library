# Film the Ambitious-Goal Commitment — Then Mine the Video for Coaching Direction, and Close the Loop at Year End

## Pattern

Students record a short video at the start of the year naming their ambitious goals — the "impossible things" they will do — addressed to their future selves and their families. The video is a commitment device *and* a diagnostic, and the Guide works it in three moves:

1. **Have the student commit on camera, specifically.** Not "I'll work hard" — named, countable goals with an audience: "I am going to build a 100-piece wiggle set… perform in front of 25 people… interview you at the end of the year."
2. **Score the video against fixed criteria and cite the evidence.** The rubric in use: enthusiasm and alignment; love of school and hard work; acknowledges doing academics faster and its benefits; specific, ambitious goals; future orientation — each scored with a written rationale quoting the student.
3. **Write what the video changes about your coaching.** Every entry ends in a coaching decision, not a rating: "I need to help Zarielle articulate how crushing her masteries in the morning will open up time and opportunity to achieve her goals."

The design also carries a **year-end second video** with its own criteria — acknowledges growth, updated ambitious goals, improved delivery mechanics, awareness of the school's role in goal achievement — so the opening commitment gets measured against a closing one.

The mechanism: an ambitious goal spoken to a camera (and to parents) is harder to quietly abandon than one written in a meeting, and the recording is a dated baseline of the student's articulation, confidence, and self-theory that no observation note captures as cheaply. The scoring criteria force the Guide past "cute video" into what the student can and can't yet articulate — which is precisely the coaching gap.

Related: BL-028 surfaces the personally meaningful goal and BL-029 calibrates it (ambitious, specific, credible) in conversation; this is the ritual artifact that makes the commitment public and reusable as a diagnostic. BL-085's visible-progress logic and BL-098's operating-instructions logic both benefit from the video as a recorded baseline.

## Evidence

*1- The commitment, in the student's own frame*

A Wonder Lab student's capsule video: "I am going to do five impossible things this year… build a 100-piece wiggle set… make a craft picnic without any help with the guides… perform a class play in front of 25 people… Mom and dad, guess what? I am going to interview you at the end of the year." Ambitious, countable, audience-bearing, and closure wired in by the student herself.

## Anti-Pattern

The anti-pattern is the unclosed capsule: goals filmed, assessed once, and never revisited — the commitment device without the reckoning.

*1- Measured in this corpus*

The datasheet's entire year-end block — acknowledges growth, updated ambitious goals, improved delivery mechanics, awareness of the school's role — is **empty for all 1,688 student rows across all 26 sheets**: 726 beginning-of-year guide entries, zero end-of-year entries. The loop the instrument was built to close did not close in SY25-26. (The same lapse shape BL-107 measures for the weekly workshop loop, and BL-092 for daily skill practice.)

Source artifact:[Ambitious Goals Student Videos](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1oa9vujtLhhyddbart-DhHt4saNRPX54xWKUU9RcJTM0/edit?usp=sharing)

*2- Filming without a usable read*

Where the ritual ran without conditions that let the student perform, guides recorded the video yielding nothing: "I didn't gain any insights from her video. She was very shy and it didn't show her true personality"; students "fed lines the entire time" by guides. A scripted or intimidating filming produces an artifact with no diagnostic value.

Source artifact:[Ambitious Goals Student Videos](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1oa9vujtLhhyddbart-DhHt4saNRPX54xWKUU9RcJTM0/edit?usp=sharing)

*3- Rubric applied past its age band*

"The components of this doc don't align with the abilities of a PreK student a few weeks into S1" — a guide flagging that the criteria were scored on students they don't fit.

## Reusable Coaching Move

1. **Film early, and coach the goals before the camera rolls** — ambitious *and* credible against the student's starting point (BL-029), in their own words rather than guide-fed lines.
2. **Address it to a real audience** — future self, parents — and let the student wire in the closure ("I'll interview you at the end of the year").
3. **Score against the criteria and quote the student** in every rationale; the quote is what makes the score inspectable.
4. **End every assessment with one coaching decision:** "what this video tells me I need to work on with this kid." No entry closes on a rating.
5. **Calendar the second video now** — same student, criteria for growth, updated goals, and delivery. An uncollected year-end video turns the whole ritual into a keepsake.

## Confidence: Medium-High for the beginning-of-year practice — 131 students, 24 guides, two campuses, all levels, with quoted evidence and coaching decisions per student; Low for the full loop as designed, since no year-end assessment was completed anywhere in the corpus and no outcome link exists between video coaching decisions and later student results.
