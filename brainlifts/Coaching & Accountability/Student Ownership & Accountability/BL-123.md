# Train the Hold-Back as Countable Reps — Quotas, Pre-Scripted Probes, and a Named Escalation Path

## Pattern

"Don't rescue students" fails as a principle because the rescue is a reflex, not a decision — the guide has synthesized the strategy or read the question aloud before any deliberation happens. The coaching that worked in this corpus did not restate the principle; it converted it into countable reps:

1. **Pre-scripted probes** — before the meeting, the guide writes 2–3 root-cause questions and one planned "stay in the discomfort" moment, so the alternative to rescuing exists before the reflex fires.
2. **A minimum-probe rule** — at least one "why" follow-up after a student shares a struggle, before offering or suggesting anything.
3. **A hold-back quota** — at least one moment per meeting where the guide consciously waits longer before stepping in; countable, and reviewable in recordings.
4. **A named escalation path** — students who genuinely cannot reach the answer are routed to a booked coaching call, so not-rescuing never becomes abandoning.

During independent work the same discipline shows up as recall prompts ("What tool do we already have for this?") instead of content delivery.

## Anti-Pattern

The immediate-fix reflex: reading questions aloud for the student, synthesizing strategies on their behalf, stepping in before students have tried. Cost documented in the corpus's coaching language: cognitive load stays with the adult, and the guide's help becomes part of how the student works.

## Reusable Coaching Move

When coaching a guide out of the rescue reflex, do not assign the principle — assign the reps:

1. **Script before the meeting** — 2–3 root-cause probes plus one planned hold-back moment, written down.
2. **One why before any solution** — minimum-probe rule after every shared struggle.
3. **Count the holds** — a per-meeting quota of deliberate waits, reviewed against meeting recordings.
4. **Name the exit** — a booked coaching call for genuinely stuck students, so the guide can hold back without guilt.

The standard is not: "The Guide believes in productive struggle." The standard is: "The Guide can show you, in this week's recording, the moment they held back on purpose — and what the student did next."
